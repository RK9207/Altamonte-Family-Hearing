\#\# Conversion model

\*\*Primary goal:\*\* a visitor books an appropriate hearing-care appointment through the supplied My Hearing Portal widget. Every button labeled \*\*Book Appointment\*\* opens that widget in an accessible popup; it does not jump to the request form.

\*\*Secondary action:\*\* call \*\*407-949-6737\*\* for questions, help choosing care, or assistance booking. Use \`tel:+14079496737\`. This is especially useful for mobile visitors, caregivers, Spanish-language care, and home-visit inquiries.

\*\*Alternate route to the same care goal:\*\* the required embedded appointment-request form for visitors who prefer to ask the clinic to arrange the next step. Label this \*\*Request an Appointment\*\*, distinguish it from direct booking, and do not promise an instantly confirmed slot. Avoid a third equal-weight action beside every booking button.

\*\*Audience priority:\*\* adults noticing everyday hearing difficulty; people dissatisfied with existing hearing aids; family members arranging care for a parent or relative. Older adults are an important audience, but the page must not imply care is only for seniors.

\*\*Decision sequence:\*\* recognize the problem → understand the first visit → see why this clinic's process is credible → know the relevant services and people → hear patient evidence → resolve objections → act with location certainty.

\#\# Top-to-bottom structure

Section IDs below are stable across all three governing documents. The header, closing invitation, and footer are small functional regions, not additional long content sections.

\#\#\# S0. Sticky header

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* establish the clinic and keep help and booking available.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “Am I in the right place, and how do I contact them?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* local Altamonte Family Hearing care with an obvious next step.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* linked logo; desktop anchors \*\*Care\*\*, \*\*Our Team\*\*, \*\*Reviews\*\*, \*\*FAQs\*\*; phone number; Book Appointment. Logo opens the clinic's main website, as required by the brief.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* recognizability and persistent orientation; the menu stays secondary to booking.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* primary booking button plus a quieter phone link. Mobile keeps the logo and booking button; calling is handled by the bottom bar.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* logo only; identity rather than decoration. No header credential strip.

\#\#\# S1. Hero — Better hearing begins with understanding your hearing

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* connect everyday conversation difficulty with a practical local first step.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “Can this clinic help with my hearing, and why should I take a closer look?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* personalized hearing care in Altamonte Springs, with clear answers and options for better everyday communication.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* benefit-led H1 that identifies hearing care; Altamonte Springs; independent/family-owned context; brief supporting copy about evaluation and explaining options; Google rating/count badge; Book Appointment. A quiet \*\*Hablamos español\*\* line may sit below the action.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* immediate relevance, location fit, and social proof without a wall of claims.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* dominant Book Appointment; subordinate call link. The request form is not in the hero.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* helpful. Prefer a real patient–provider conversation in the clinic, with usable faces and space for the composition. Role: warmth plus evidence of real care. A real team photo is the fallback. Keep text outside the photo.

\#\#\# S2. Recognition — When everyday listening takes more effort

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* help people recognize a reason to seek an evaluation without self-diagnosing.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “Does what I am experiencing warrant getting checked?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* difficulty catching words or using hearing aids comfortably is a useful reason to understand your hearing.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* asking people to repeat; hearing speech without catching words; struggling in restaurants or family conversations; TV volume; ringing/buzzing; existing hearing aids that are not working well. End with reassurance that not every concern requires hearing aids.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* recognition without shame, alarm, or a sales funnel disguised as a diagnosis. Include a visible, calm safety note: sudden hearing loss, or tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo, requires immediate medical care rather than waiting for this appointment.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* no new button cluster. A brief transition leads into what the visit involves; header remains available.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* not needed. Plain symptom rows or a two-column list are clearer than generic lifestyle tiles. Small consistent icons may aid scanning but cannot carry meaning alone.

\#\#\# S3. First visit — Understand your hearing and your options

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* make an unfamiliar appointment concrete and reduce fear of being sold a device.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “What will happen, and will I have to buy hearing aids?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* concerns are heard, ears and hearing are checked, and results and suitable next steps are explained.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* four stages: discuss history and daily challenges; examine ears/check for wax; test hearing in the sound booth including tones and speech; explain results and options during the visit. If hearing aids are appropriate, recommendations account for hearing needs, lifestyle, budget, and preferences. Most hearing evaluations are complimentary; call to confirm any costs that may apply.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* process transparency, reduced financial uncertainty, and informed choice. Never imply all services happen at the first visit or promise a duration.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* first in-body repeat of Book Appointment after the process and qualified cost statement.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* helpful. Real sound-booth or consultation photo. Role: explanation and familiarity. Use a caption tied to what is actually visible.

\#\#\# S4. Difference — Hearing care checked for your ears, with support afterward

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* explain meaningful reasons to choose this practice, beyond generic friendliness.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “What is different here, especially if hearing aids have disappointed me before?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* individualized verification and ongoing professional care matter as much as selecting a device.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* plain-language Real Ear Measurement (REM) explanation; Electroacoustic Analysis (EAA) checks device performance before fitting; Jaysee's HearingUp Certified Provider status; fitting recommendations across six manufacturers; five years of professional care included with hearing aids purchased from this office, at every technology level. Keep that scope beside the five-year statement.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* demonstrates the mechanism behind care quality; answers skepticism and after-purchase anxiety. Describe actual processes, not guaranteed outcomes or superiority over every competitor.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* one Book Appointment after the complete section. A source link explaining HearingUp can be a quiet optional reference, not a competing promotion.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* helpful. Real REM procedure image alongside the explanation, with an accurate caption. Role: credible proof of method. A simple labeled illustration is a fallback only if it is clinically accurate and clearly illustrative.

\#\#\# S5. Care options — Support for the hearing concern you have now

\- \*\*Purpose:\*\* establish service fit without making the page a product catalog.  
\- \*\*Visitor question:\*\* “Do they handle my specific concern, including aids bought elsewhere?”  
\- \*\*Main message:\*\* evaluation, fitting, maintenance, and related hearing care are available in one practice.  
\- \*\*Key information:\*\* six concise service entries: hearing evaluations; hearing-aid fitting and adjustment; repair/cleaning for devices bought here or elsewhere; tinnitus consultation; earwax removal when appropriate; mobile/home hearing care. REM belongs within fitting and S4, not in a seventh duplicate tile. Explain weekday walk-in cleaning/minor-repair hour and home-visit availability by phone. Preserve tinnitus, earwax, and mobile-testing limitations beside those entries or in linked FAQ answers.  
\- \*\*Trust/CRO role:\*\* relevance for varied needs, especially returning hearing-aid users and caregivers; demonstrates support beyond a purchase.  
\- \*\*CTA role:\*\* no button per tile. The mobile/home entry can say \*\*Call about home visits\*\*. All other entries support the common booking path.  
\- \*\*Imagery:\*\* optional. Do not force six stock photographs into a grid. A real repair-bench detail can accompany the section if it explains care; otherwise typography and small icons suffice.

\#\# Placement and repetition decisions

| Element | Placement | Reason |  
|---|---|---|  
| Book Appointment | Header, hero, after S3, after S4, after S7, S11 | Ready visitors can act immediately; deliberative visitors see another action after uncertainty, method, and patient proof are resolved. |  
| Phone | Desktop header, hero secondary link, home-visit entry, FAQ support line, form fallback, location, closing/footer; mobile bottom bar | Provides help at meaningful friction points without making a second dominant button at every section. |  
| Google rating/count | S1 and S7 | Immediate legitimacy, then fuller evidence; both use one consistent dated source. |  
| Credentials and certification | S4 and S6 | Method first, then the qualified person behind it; keep explanations attached to badges. |  
| Five years of professional care | S4; optional short reinforcement in relevant FAQ | After-purchase reassurance with purchase scope visible; not a universal warranty. |  
| Dedicated reviews | S7 after providers | Readers know whose care and what process the testimonials describe. |  
| Request form | S9 after FAQs | Alternate appointment path after questions are addressed, not a competing hero funnel. |  
| Map/location | S10 after form, before closing invitation | Local certainty without pulling early visitors out of the main conversion sequence. |

Do not auto-open booking on arrival, after a timer, or on exit. Do not use appointment scarcity, countdowns, fabricated urgency, or an automatic review carousel.

\#\# Layering useful information

Keep REM understandable in S4; put deeper terminology in a caption or expandable explanation. Include six manufacturer names in S4/S5 or the relevant FAQ; omit long current-model catalogs. Place adjustment expectations, wax screening, tinnitus limits, and home-testing differences in relevant service entries and FAQ answers. Full review text can expand within the selected cards. Supporting provider association memberships can sit below bios without becoming a separate section.

Do not create standalone sections for device styles, every manufacturer, general hearing-loss science, awards, the owner's podcast, or six repeated benefit tiles. Those would dilute the care decision. Their useful fragments can remain in the richer Step 4 draft at the point where they answer a real visitor question. Product features such as rechargeability and Bluetooth can be a short optional sentence, never the main pitch.

\#\# Mobile priority

1\. Logo, readable benefit/H1, location, Google proof, and Book Appointment precede a large photo. Do not force every hero item into a single screen at the expense of legibility.  
2\. Keep the mobile header to logo and booking. The bottom \*\*Call\*\* bar is the quieter, persistent assisted route, with primary-green background and pure-white text/icon.  
3\. Maintain the same informational sequence in one column. Number the visit steps vertically; explain REM before its photo; keep provider name and credentials together.  
4\. Show the three strongest selected review cards in a simple vertical list. Do not conceal another three in a carousel or load-more control on mobile; route to Google for the rest.  
5\. Allow natural FAQ expansion and form height. The page must scroll rather than cut off iframe content or force horizontal scrolling.  
6\. Reserve bottom safe-area space for the call bar. Hide it when the booking dialog is open; prevent it from covering active form controls or the software keyboard.  
7\. Keep address and hours as text outside the map. Give Get Directions a full accessible label and a comfortable touch target.

\#\# Measurement and review gates for the later build

Measure booking-button clicks, widget load failures, phone clicks, request-form visibility, direction clicks, and review-link clicks. Booking and form completion require a documented integration from their cross-origin vendors; do not count popup opening as a completed appointment or invent iframe events. Do not collect sensitive free-text or patient details in analytics.

Before release, validate both embed routes, all contact links, actual Google count, supplied imagery, claim scopes, mobile review limits, keyboard navigation, and sticky elements. A failed widget must offer a phone fallback, never a fake booking success.

Meaningful departures in Step 5—changing the conversion route, removing substantive information, changing section order or major visual direction, replacing real clinic imagery with generated imagery, or altering required review/form behavior—must be explained and approved by the user before adoption. Routine spacing, copy clarity, and accessibility fixes within this foundation can proceed. Record any approved change in all affected governing documents so the rebuild and documents remain aligned.
