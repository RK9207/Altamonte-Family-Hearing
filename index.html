Does Any of This Sound Familiar?

Hearing changes can be gradual. If these experiences sound familiar, a hearing evaluation can help you understand why.

  • Asking for repeats

    You often need someone to say something again.

  • Missing the words

    You can hear someone talking, but you don’t catch the words.

  • Working harder in noise

    Restaurants, parties, car rides, or family gatherings take effort and can leave you tired.

  • Turning up the volume

    Other people say the TV or phone is too loud.

  • Ringing or buzzing

    Ringing, buzzing, humming, or hissing may accompany a change in hearing.

  • Aids that aren’t helping

    Your hearing aids never sounded quite right, or they’re sitting in a drawer.

Not every hearing concern calls for hearing aids. If your hearing is within normal limits, we’ll tell you.

Sudden changes need medical care first Sudden hearing loss, or tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo, is a medical emergency. Seek immediate medical care and don’t wait for a routine hearing evaluation.

Your First Visit, Explained

  1. We start by listening

    Tell us where listening is difficult. We’ll discuss your hearing and health history, including medications that may be relevant.

  2. We check your ears

    We examine your ear canals with an otoscope and check for wax. If removal is needed, we’ll explain whether we can safely help or recommend another provider.

  3. We test your hearing

    In the sound booth, you’ll listen to tones at different pitches and speech in quiet and background noise.

  4. We explain your options

    We explain your audiogram during the same visit and give you a take-home record. Together, we review options for your hearing, lifestyle, preferences, and budget.

Care That Goes Beyond the Hearing Aid

If hearing aids are right for you, we verify your fitting and provide care that continues after your first visit.

Measured in your ear

Real Ear Measurement checks the sound your hearing aid delivers against targets from your own hearing test. Those measurements guide adjustments to your fitting.

Device checked before fitting

Electroacoustic Analysis checks the hearing aid itself against the manufacturer’s specifications before every fitting. It is a separate check from your hearing test and in-ear measurement.

Explore how we verify your fitting

Real Ear Measurement (REM)

A thin probe-microphone tube sits in your ear canal beside the hearing aid. It measures sound near your eardrum at soft, average and loud levels. We compare those measurements with targets calculated from your own hearing test, then fine-tune your fitting.

Two people with the same hearing test results, hearing aids and software settings can receive very different sound in their ear canals. Software defaults use average ear-canal values; REM checks what reaches your individual ear. It verifies the fitting and cannot promise perfect hearing.

Electroacoustic Analysis (EAA)

A separate test-box check measures the device’s output, amplification and distortion against the manufacturer’s specifications before every fitting. It checks device performance, rather than your hearing, and does not replace Real Ear Measurement.

Hearing Care for Your Next Step

Help with new hearing concerns, existing hearing aids, or care for a family member.

Hearing evaluations

Ear checks and sound-booth testing for adults of all ages. Most evaluations are complimentary; call to confirm any costs that may apply.

Hearing aid fittings

A verified fitting and follow-up adjustments tailored to your needs. Hearing aids do not restore natural hearing or remove all background noise.

Fitting details

Options reflect your hearing, lifestyle, preferences and budget. We program your hearing aids to targets calculated from your hearing test and verify the fitting with Real Ear Measurement. Follow-up visits help fine-tune your fit. The adjustment period varies; familiar sounds and your own voice may seem different at first.

Repairs and cleaning

Support for most major hearing-aid brands, including aids bought elsewhere. Some repairs need factory service; same-day repair is not guaranteed.

Repair and maintenance details

We troubleshoot, clean, program and repair hearing aids. Many small repairs—battery doors, receivers, tubing, domes and filters—can be handled in our office. We also use professional drying for moisture. Moisture inside the case, a cracked shell or a failed circuit board may require factory service, which we coordinate with the manufacturer.

A professional deep cleaning about every six months is recommended to help address moisture and earwax before they cause a repair.

Tinnitus consultation

Hearing evaluation and options for managing tinnitus. There is no universal cure; relief varies and specialized referral may be needed.

Tinnitus options

We discuss ways to reduce the impact of ringing, buzzing, hissing or pulsing sounds. When hearing loss is also present, hearing aids with built-in sound-therapy features may help make tinnitus less noticeable. We refer you to other providers for specialized tinnitus care when appropriate.

Earwax removal

Ear checks and gentle warm-water removal. Suitability is checked first: irrigation is not right for every ear, and an ENT referral may be needed.

Screening details

We examine your ears with a video otoscope, so you can see the image on screen. Ear surgery, ear tubes, a perforated eardrum or a recent infection can mean irrigation is unsuitable. We screen first and refer to an ENT when needed. Suitable ears can be treated with warmed water at controlled low pressure.

Answers before you book

Straight answers on cost, insurance, your current hearing aids and what our care covers.

Is there a charge for a hearing evaluation?

Most hearing evaluations are complimentary. Call our office at 407-949-6737 for details and to confirm any costs that may apply.

Do you accept insurance?

We accept many insurance plans, but coverage for hearing aids varies from plan to plan. Bring your insurance card to your visit and we'll check your benefits for you, or call us with your plan details beforehand.

Will I need hearing aids?

Not every hearing concern calls for hearing aids. After your evaluation, we explain your results and the options that may suit you. If your hearing is within normal limits, we'll tell you.

Can you help with hearing aids I bought somewhere else?

Yes. We clean, troubleshoot, program and repair hearing aids from most major manufacturers, including ones purchased from other providers, and we can verify a fitting with Real Ear Measurement.

Some problems, such as moisture inside the case, a cracked shell or a failed circuit board, need factory repair, which we coordinate with the manufacturer. Our walk-in hour for cleanings and minor repairs is Monday to Friday, 1:00 to 2:00 pm.

How long does it take to get used to new hearing aids?

It varies from person to person. Familiar sounds and your own voice may seem different at first. Regular use and follow-up visits help as you adjust.

Can hearing aids help with tinnitus?

When tinnitus comes with hearing loss, hearing aids, including models with built-in sound-therapy features, may help make it less noticeable. There is no universal cure for tinnitus, and the amount of relief varies. We refer you to other providers for specialized tinnitus care when needed.

If your tinnitus started suddenly, is in one ear only, or pulses in time with your heartbeat, see a physician promptly. Tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo is a medical emergency.

What if my problem is just earwax?

Symptoms alone can't tell you whether wax is the cause, which is why we look in your ears before testing. We'll explain whether wax can be removed safely in our office or whether you should see another provider.

Please avoid cotton swabs in the ear canal, which can push wax deeper. Ear candling doesn't reliably remove earwax and can cause burns or other injuries.

Do you offer home visits?

Yes. Toni provides hearing evaluations and hearing aid fittings at your home or care facility for people who have difficulty getting to the office, in the Greater Orlando area. Call us for cost and availability. Some tests require in-office equipment, so home testing may not include everything a sound-booth evaluation can.

Can my visit be in Spanish?

Sí. Ofrecemos evaluaciones auditivas, formularios y recursos en español. Jaysee is bilingual, and your whole visit can be in Spanish. Our Patient Care Coordinators, Grace and Francesca, are bilingual in English and Spanish too.

Who will provide my care, and are you part of a chain?

We're an independent, family-owned and operated practice, not part of a chain. You'll see Jaysee Soto (HAS, BC-HIS) or Toni Trager (HAS), both Florida-licensed Hearing Aid Specialists. They are not audiologists or physicians, and they refer you to a physician, ENT or other specialist when your care falls outside their scope.

Grace Soto and Francesca Natal, our Patient Care Coordinators, help with scheduling and forms.

What should I do if my hearing changes suddenly?

Sudden hearing loss, or tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo, is a medical emergency. Seek immediate medical care. Don't wait for a routine hearing evaluation.
