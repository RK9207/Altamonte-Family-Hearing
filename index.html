When listening takes more effort, it's worth finding out why

Hearing changes are often gradual and easy to miss day to day. If any of these sound familiar, a hearing evaluation can help you understand what's going on, without assuming you'll need hearing aids.

Often it's a family member who notices first. A family member or friend can come along to help review your results.

  • You often ask people to repeat themselves.
  • You can tell someone is talking, but you don't catch the words.
  • Conversations in restaurants, at parties, in the car or at family gatherings take real effort, and you may feel tired afterward.
  • Other people say the TV or phone is too loud.
  • You notice ringing, buzzing, humming or hissing, sometimes along with a change in hearing.
  • Your hearing aids never sounded quite right, or they're sitting in a drawer.

Not every hearing concern calls for hearing aids. If your hearing is within normal limits, we'll tell you.

Sudden changes need medical care first Sudden hearing loss, or tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo, is a medical emergency. Seek immediate medical care and don't wait for a routine hearing evaluation.

Your first visit: understand your hearing, then your options

  1. We start by listening

    Tell us what's been difficult and where: restaurants, family gatherings, the phone, the TV. We'll also ask about your hearing and health history and any medications, since these can be relevant to changes in hearing or tinnitus.

  2. We look in your ears

    Before testing, we examine your ear canals with an otoscope and check for earwax. If wax is in the way, we'll explain whether it's safe to remove in our office or whether you should see another provider.

  3. You take a hearing test

    In our sound booth, you'll listen to tones at different pitches, then to speech in quiet and in background noise.

  4. We explain your results and next steps

    During the same visit, we review your audiogram in plain terms, and you take home a record you can compare at future visits. Then we talk through options based on your hearing, lifestyle, preferences and budget.

A fitting checked for your ears, with care afterward

A hearing aid is only as effective as the care behind it. Here is what we do, and why it matters, especially if hearing aids have disappointed you before.

Real Ear Measurement (REM)

Two people can have the same hearing test results, the same hearing aids and the same software settings, and still receive very different sound in their ear canals. Software defaults are based on average ear-canal values. Your ears may not be average.

With Real Ear Measurement, a thin probe-microphone tube is placed in your ear canal next to the hearing aid. It measures the sound actually delivered near your eardrum at soft, average and loud levels. We compare that with targets calculated from your own hearing test, so your fitting can be verified, and fine-tuned, for your ear.

REM is verification. It is not a promise of perfect hearing.

Electroacoustic Analysis (EAA)

Before every fitting, we also run a separate test-box check. Electroacoustic Analysis measures the hearing aid's own output, amplification and distortion against the manufacturer's specifications. It checks the device, not your hearing, and it doesn't replace Real Ear Measurement.

HearingUp Certified Provider

Jaysee is a HearingUp Certified Provider. HearingUp is a provider network founded by Dr. Cliff Olson. Its providers are personally reviewed and commit to best practices, including fitting verification with Real Ear Measurement.

Hearing aid fittings and adjustments

Recommendations based on your hearing test, lifestyle, preferences and budget. Your hearing aids are programmed to targets calculated from your hearing test, verified with Real Ear Measurement, and fine-tuned at follow-up visits.

Hearing aids may help, but they don't restore natural hearing, and no hearing aid removes background noise completely. The adjustment period varies, and familiar sounds and your own voice may seem different at first.

Hearing aid repair and cleaning

We troubleshoot, clean, program and repair hearing aids from most major manufacturers, including hearing aids you bought elsewhere. Many small repairs, such as battery doors, receivers, tubing, domes and filters, can be handled in our office, with professional drying for moisture.

Moisture inside the case, a cracked shell or a failed circuit board may need factory service, which we coordinate with the manufacturer. Same-day repair isn't guaranteed for every issue.

Tinnitus consultation

Ringing, buzzing, hissing or pulsing? We evaluate your hearing and talk through ways to reduce the impact. When hearing loss is also present, hearing aids with built-in sound-therapy features may help make tinnitus less noticeable.

There is no universal cure, and relief varies from person to person. We refer you to other providers for specialized tinnitus care when that's appropriate.

Earwax removal

We examine your ears with a video otoscope, so you can see the image on screen, and, when it's appropriate, gently remove wax with warm-water irrigation at controlled low pressure.

We screen first. Ear surgery, ear tubes, a perforated eardrum or a recent infection can mean irrigation isn't suitable, and we would refer you to an ENT.

Answers before you book

Straight answers on cost, insurance, your current hearing aids and what our care covers.

Is there a charge for a hearing evaluation?

Most hearing evaluations are complimentary. Call our office at 407-949-6737 for details and to confirm any costs that may apply.

Do you accept insurance?

We accept many insurance plans, but coverage for hearing aids varies from plan to plan. Bring your insurance card to your visit and we'll check your benefits for you, or call us with your plan details beforehand.

Will I need hearing aids?

Not every hearing concern calls for hearing aids. After your evaluation, we explain your results and the options that may suit you. If your hearing is within normal limits, we'll tell you.

Can you help with hearing aids I bought somewhere else?

Yes. We clean, troubleshoot, program and repair hearing aids from most major manufacturers, including ones purchased from other providers, and we can verify a fitting with Real Ear Measurement.

Some problems, such as moisture inside the case, a cracked shell or a failed circuit board, need factory repair, which we coordinate with the manufacturer. Our walk-in hour for cleanings and minor repairs is Monday to Friday, 1:00 to 2:00 pm.

How long does it take to get used to new hearing aids?

It varies from person to person. Familiar sounds and your own voice may seem different at first. Regular use and follow-up visits help as you adjust.

Can hearing aids help with tinnitus?

When tinnitus comes with hearing loss, hearing aids, including models with built-in sound-therapy features, may help make it less noticeable. There is no universal cure for tinnitus, and the amount of relief varies. We refer you to other providers for specialized tinnitus care when needed.

If your tinnitus started suddenly, is in one ear only, or pulses in time with your heartbeat, see a physician promptly. Tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo is a medical emergency.

What if my problem is just earwax?

Symptoms alone can't tell you whether wax is the cause, which is why we look in your ears before testing. We'll explain whether wax can be removed safely in our office or whether you should see another provider.

Please avoid cotton swabs in the ear canal, which can push wax deeper. Ear candling doesn't reliably remove earwax and can cause burns or other injuries.

Do you offer home visits?

Yes. Toni provides hearing evaluations and hearing aid fittings at your home or care facility for people who have difficulty getting to the office, in the Greater Orlando area. Call us for cost and availability. Some tests require in-office equipment, so home testing may not include everything a sound-booth evaluation can.

Can my visit be in Spanish?

Sí. Ofrecemos evaluaciones auditivas, formularios y recursos en español. Jaysee is bilingual, and your whole visit can be in Spanish. Our Patient Care Coordinators, Grace and Francesca, are bilingual in English and Spanish too.

Who will provide my care, and are you part of a chain?

We're an independent, family-owned and operated practice, not part of a chain. You'll see Jaysee Soto (HAS, BC-HIS) or Toni Trager (HAS), both Florida-licensed Hearing Aid Specialists. They are not audiologists or physicians, and they refer you to a physician, ENT or other specialist when your care falls outside their scope.

Grace Soto and Francesca Natal, our Patient Care Coordinators, help with scheduling and forms.

What should I do if my hearing changes suddenly?

Sudden hearing loss, or tinnitus with sudden severe vertigo, is a medical emergency. Seek immediate medical care. Don't wait for a routine hearing evaluation.
